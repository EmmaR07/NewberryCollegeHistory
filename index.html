How It All Began

In 1856, the Rev John Bachman, the principal founder of Newberry College, chartered the institutioin as a college, seminary, and preparatory school of the Lutheran Synod of South Carolina.

The Civil War had a significant impact on the amount of students at Newberry College, as in 1861, many students resigned from classes to fight in the Confederate Army.

Because of the war, the college was forced to relocate to Walhalla, South Carolina, taking the College bell and a few books with them, making the most of what they could.

In 1969, the first bachelor's degree was awarded, and 8 years later, the college returned to its original site in the middle of Newberry, South Carolina.

Time for a Change

In 1885, Newberry College began offering masters degrees, and only 2 years later, they started accepting women students.

In 1898, sitting president George B. Cromer, the first Alumni to become college president, met with the countrys president to discuss repercussioins regarding damage to the college during the CCivil War. Because of this meeting, the college was awarded with $15,000 which was later put towards the bulding of what is now Holland Hall.

In 1899, baseball became the first official intercollegiate sport. Since the players wore bright red uniforms, they were called the "Red Men" later being changed to the Indians.

The Merging of Two Institutions

Newberry College became fully coeducational through a merger with Summerland College, an institution located only 30 miles away. Due to such a short distance, the college decided to fully merge both colleges into one, creating for a greater education. This included the addition of many more women faculty and students, as well as the addition of music and arts programs.

Newberry College Today

Today, Newberry College continues to provide a high-quality education while maintaining its commitment to academic excellence and student development. The college now offers twelve fully online bachelors degrees and four graduate degree programs.
